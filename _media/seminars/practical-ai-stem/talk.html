HANDOUT p. 6LESSON PLANNING | TWO READY-TO-USE PROMPTS

Let AI ask you questions

Paste this prompt unchanged, then answer the questions as they appear.

  • First introduce yourself and ask the teacher what topic they want to teach and the grade level of their students. Wait for the teacher to respond. Do not move on until the teacher responds.
  • Next ask the teacher if students have existing knowledge about the topic or if this is an entirely new topic.
  • Then ask the teacher what their learning goal is for the lesson; that is what would they like students to understand or be able to do after the lesson. Wait for a response.
  • Ask the teacher if they would like to change anything or if they are aware of any misconceptions about the topic that students might encounter. Wait for a response.

HANDOUT p. 7QUIZZES | SOURCE MATERIAL AND DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS

Ethan and Lilach Mollick: interactive Quiz Creator

The AI asks the teacher two setup questions per turn and waits for answers.

  • First turn: The AI asks what topic you teach and your students’ learning level.
  • Next turn: After you reply, the AI asks what students should understand and what difficulties they may have.
  • Then it drafts the whole quiz: 5 multiple-choice questions and 2 open-ended questions, ordered from easiest to most difficult, plus an answer key.
  • Check both recall and application, covering the topic as well as common difficulties.
  • Use plausible incorrect choices, avoid “all of the above,” and phrase questions positively.

Summary of the prompt’s sequence. The two-question limit applies to the teacher interview. Full prompt: handout page 7.

HANDOUT p. 8PERSONALIZED ASSIGNMENTS | SUPPORT, CHALLENGE, AND INTEREST

Create Scaffolds and Extensions

Adapt the same learning goal.

Scaffolds (2-3 options):

Create supports that remove specific barriers without lowering cognitive demand.

  • Available to any student who needs them (not labeled for specific groups)
  • Gradually releasable as students build skills
  • Focused on access, not doing the thinking for students

Extensions (2-3 options):

Create opportunities for deeper or broader thinking that increase complexity without just adding more work.

  • Elevate the cognitive demand or application
  • Be authentic challenges, not just "extra" or "bonus"
  • Connect to the same core objectives

Connect feedback to your criteria

For a paragraph, essay, lab report, or written explanation: replace the bracketed fields and attach your instructions, criteria, and the student’s work.

I have attached the following assessment details: [assessment instructions], [rubric].

The assessment measures the following learning objective(s): [insert objective(s)].

Act as an assistant for drafting student feedback. Review the attached [anonymized student submission].

For each rubric criterion, identify relevant evidence, one strength, and one area for development. Do not assign a score or infer anything not stated in the submission. Flag uncertain judgments for instructor review. If an attachment is missing or unreadable, ask for it before proceeding.
